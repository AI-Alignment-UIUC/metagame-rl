# Tempo card value in the 2000 Pokemon TCG metagame

Tags: [read] = page fetched and read; [snippet] = seen only in search results; [derived] = my arithmetic from printed card text; [memory] = not sourced, flagged for checking.

**Bottom line for the project:** I could not reach any 2000-era strategy writing. pojo.com (April 2000 Haymaker article, Team Rocket history) and Bulbapedia returned HTTP 403, and the Pokebeach thread and Haymaker blog returned 403/404. So the "why players valued it" claims below come from later retro-format writers, not from 2000. The card text and HP numbers are solid. The Energy-count and tournament-rules claims are thinner.

## Q1. Dominant decks and HP/damage numbers

### Takeaway
Haymaker (Hitmonchan, Electabuzz, Scyther, with Basics around 70 HP) is the deck the sources name as the Base-Fossil-era archetype. Its Trainer-heavy build is the one in the sources I read.

### Cited Findings
- Base Set Hitmonchan: 70 HP, Fighting, Weakness Psychic x2, retreat 2; Jab {F} 20; Special Punch {F}{F}{C} 40 — [pkmncards](https://pkmncards.com/card/hitmonchan-base-set-bs-7/) [read]
- Base Set Electabuzz: 70 HP, Lightning, Weakness Fighting x2, retreat 2; Thundershock {L} 10 + paralysis flip; Thunderpunch {L}{C} 30 (+10 on heads, 10 self-damage on tails) — [pkmncards](https://pkmncards.com/card/electabuzz-base-set-bs-20/) [read]
- Haymaker sample list (12 Pokemon / 25 Trainers / 23 Energy): 4 Hitmonchan, 4 Electabuzz, 4 Scyther; 4 PlusPower, 4 Energy Removal, 4 Super Energy Removal, 3 Gust of Wind, 3 Professor Oak, 3 Bill, 2 Item Finder, 2 Scoop Up; 10 Fighting, 9 Electric, 4 Double Colorless — [PokeBase answer, Feb 2012](https://pokemondb.net/pokebase/57873/what-is-a-haymaker-tcg-deck) [read; a 2012 Q&A, not a 2000 list]
- Haymaker variant list: 2 Electabuzz, 4 Hitmonchan, 4 Jigglypuff, 4 PlusPower, 4 Gust of Wind — [Pokebeach thread](https://www.pokebeach.com/forums/threads/unlimited-wave-maker-wigglytuff-hitmonchan.100835) [snippet only; page 403]
- Haymaker "has a high chance of playing a solid game and taking knock outs through the beginning turns"; default Trainer set "3 Professor Oak, 2 Computer Search, 2 Item Finder, 2 Gust of Wind and 2 Super Energy Removal" — [Evolution Enthusiast, Aug 2024](https://evolutionenthusiast.blogspot.com/2024/08/prop-153-base-gym-deck-profile-haymaker.html) [read; modern retro writer]
- Same blog calls the format "1999-2003 Retro" with a "15 Trainer limitation" and says Haymaker was "the best deck in format" in the original game — [same](https://evolutionenthusiast.blogspot.com/2024/08/prop-153-base-gym-deck-profile-haymaker.html) [read; secondhand claim]
- Rain Dance Blastoise, Wigglytuff, Mewtwo, Damn Fossil, Muk: named by the user; I found no source confirming their 2000 standings. [gap]

### Inferences
- [derived] Electabuzz Thunderpunch vs a Fighting-Weakness Basic (x2): base 30 becomes 60. With PlusPower, (30+10) x2 = 80. On a 70-HP Basic that is the 60 -> 80 crossing: no KO becomes a one-hit KO. Heads on the coin adds the same +10 before doubling, so a flipped coin also crosses 70.
- [derived] Hitmonchan Special Punch (40) against a 70-HP non-Weakness Basic needs 2 hits. With PlusPower it is 50, still 2 hits. So PlusPower alone does not OHKO a neutral 70-HP target from Hitmonchan; it does turn Weakness hits into OHKOs. This is why Gust of Wind is the other half of the combo.

### Gaps
- No 2000 decklists or tournament reports read. pojo "Killer Decks" and Wizards reports are 403 or not reached.
- Who dominated mid-to-late 2000 is not established from a source I could read.

## Q3. Gust of Wind usage

### Takeaway
Gust of Wind is a switch: it drags up a benched Pokemon, so the card works only with a damaged or Weakness target on the bench. Writers paired it with Electabuzz (Weakness) and Energy Removal.

### Cited Findings
- Card text: "Choose 1 of your opponent's Benched Pokémon and switch it with his or her Active Pokémon." Base Set 93, released Jan 9, 1999 — [pkmncards](https://pkmncards.com/card/gust-of-wind-base-set-bs-93/) [read]
- "The standard play is Gust of Wind paired with PlusPower or Energy Removal to take an easy early Prize" — [Haymaker World blog](https://haymakerworldtcg.squarespace.com/blog/what-is-a-haymaker-deck) [snippet only; page 404]
- Haymaker Gust of Wind count: 2-4 per list across sources above — [PokeBase](https://pokemondb.net/pokebase/57873/what-is-a-haymaker-tcg-deck) [read]
- Old-format Gust recursion: "two Gust of Wind" supported by Computer Search and Item Finder — [pojo 2008 column](https://www.pojo.com/COTD/2008/Nov/14.shtml) [snippet only; page 403]

### Inferences
- Gust is decisive when the opponent has an exposed benched Pokemon with the Weakness or damage needed, and it is weak against a deck that keeps no bench.

### Gaps
- No match records or 2000-era Gust usage stats.

## Q4. Item Finder and Scoop Up

### Takeaway
Scoop Up is a reset that returns a Basic and discards everything attached to it. Item Finder recursion is in the sources but its text is not read.

### Cited Findings
- Scoop Up (Base Set 78, Rare, Jan 9, 1999): "Choose 1 of your Pokémon in play and return its Basic Pokémon card to your hand. (Discard all cards attached to that card.)" — [pkmncards](https://pkmncards.com/card/scoop-up-base-set-bs-78/) [read]
- Commenter on the same page: evolution cards are discarded with the Basic — [pkmncards](https://pkmncards.com/card/scoop-up-base-set-bs-78/) [read; a commenter, not a rules source]
- Haymaker lists run 2 Item Finder and 2 Scoop Up — [PokeBase](https://pokemondb.net/pokebase/57873/what-is-a-haymaker-tcg-deck) [read]
- Item Finder (Base Set 103) and Gust of Wind recursion via Computer Search: [pojo 2008](https://www.pojo.com/COTD/2008/Nov/14.shtml) [snippet only]
- Item Finder text: the pkmncards page returned 404. [memory] Item Finder discards a Trainer from hand to return a Trainer from the discard pile; verify before use.

### Inferences
- [derived] Scoop Up on a damaged Basic returns it and its Energy, so it converts lost Energy into a reset, which is worth a slot against Energy-heavy decks.

### Gaps
- No 2000 writer explaining why Scoop Up was worth a slot.

## Q5. Energy counts and Energy Removal pressure

### Takeaway
Haymaker sources show 23 Energy in a 60-card list with 4 Energy Removal and 4 Super Energy Removal in play. That conflicts with the project's human median of about 18 Energy. The "fewer Energy" argument is only the 2012/2024 writers' claim that few Pokemon and Energy leave room for Trainers.

### Cited Findings
- 23 Energy in the 2012 Haymaker list; 12 Pokemon and 25 Trainers — [PokeBase](https://pokemondb.net/pokebase/57873/what-is-a-haymaker-tcg-deck) [read]
- "Plays ... plenty of Trainer cards" because it runs few Pokemon and Energy — [PokeBase](https://pokemondb.net/pokebase/57873/what-is-a-haymaker-tcg-deck) [read; 2012]
- Retro Base-Fossil sample lists: 15-16 Energy in many builds — [BelleCON wiki snippet / retro blog](https://bellecon.fandom.com/wiki/File:TCG-TeamRocket.gif?oldid=4036) [snippet only; one source is low quality]
- Super Energy Removal is a default Trainer in the modern Haymaker profile — [Evolution Enthusiast](https://evolutionenthusiast.blogspot.com/2024/08/prop-153-base-gym-deck-profile-haymaker.html) [read]

### Inferences
- The 23-Energy Haymaker list and the 15-16 Energy retro lists disagree. Ask which list the project's 18-Energy median came from before using either as ground truth. Haymaker may be a low-Energy deck; the 23-Energy figure is one 2012 answer.

### Gaps
- No 2000-era strategy text on Energy counts. Energy Removal pressure is not quantified in any source I read.

## Q6. Tournament structure (2000)

### Takeaway
2000 Super Trainer Showdown West Coast: 60-card decks, 6 Prizes, single game, 25-minute limit, Swiss. East Coast (Nov 2000): Prop 15 (15 Trainers, max 3 per card). These come from snippets, not fully read.

### Cited Findings
- West Coast STS, Long Beach, July 22, 2000: Swiss, single game, 60 cards, 6 Prizes, 25-minute limit, proxy cards banned — [pokumon / pokemonaaah archive](https://pokumon.com/super-trainer-showdown-2000/) [snippet only]
- East Coast STS, Secaucus NJ (Nov 2000; one source says Nov 8, the other Nov 18-19): Prop 15, 15 Trainers, max 3 of any card except Basic Energy — [archive snippet](https://alexwilsonnc.github.io/tes/major-events/2000/nj-super-trainer-showdown/summary) [snippet only]
- Prop 15 described as a "15 Trainer limitation" — [Evolution Enthusiast](https://evolutionenthusiast.blogspot.com/2024/08/prop-153-base-gym-deck-profile-haymaker.html) [read; modern label]
- Wikipedia TCG article: no 2000 time limits, Prize counts or match formats. Its Prize and best-of-three text is current rules. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Trading_Card_Game) [read; no 2000 data]

### Inferences
- A 25-minute single game with 6 Prizes favors fast Basic decks that take Prizes early. That is the tempo context for PlusPower and Gust.
- The 15-Trainer Prop limit is a later restriction. Haymaker's 25-Trainer 2012 list is not legal under Prop 15, so the 2012 list is not a 2000-tournament list.

### Gaps
- Official Wizards 2000 rules were not found. The West Coast and East Coast details rest on fan archives.
- "Super Trainer Showdown" rules for time and Prize counts are from one snippet each.
