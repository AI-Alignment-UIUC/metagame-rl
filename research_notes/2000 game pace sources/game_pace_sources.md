# 2000 Pokemon TCG game pace: sources for "six Prizes in about 7 turns"

STATUS: INCOMPLETE. All pojo.com pages returned HTTP 403 to WebFetch (www and non-www), and web.archive.org was denied by the permission system (not retried another way). I could NOT read any of the three sources in full. Everything about them below comes from WebSearch result summaries (a small model's paraphrase of the page, with short quotes), so treat it as unverified until someone reads the pages in a browser. No game-by-game turn or minute data was found anywhere.

## 1. The three sources: exact context

### Takeaway
Two of the three could not be checked, and the third (P6) was not surfaced at all. The search summaries say the P4/P2 quote comes from Wigglytuff deck write-ups (not Haymaker) and is a capability claim, not a logged game. The "7 turns" appears to be a "get 6 KOs in 7 turns" goal under ideal conditions.

### Cited Findings
- A search for the exact P4 phrase returned both [P2 (Aug 2000, 22a)](https://www.pojo.com/KillerDecks/2000/August2000/22a.htm) and [P4 (Jun 2000, 21u)](https://www.pojo.com/KillerDecks/2000/June00/21u.htm); the summary says the phrase sits in the deck description: "with pluspowers you take out the 70 HP hay pokemon in one hit, and Item finders can get back the pluspowers if necessary to get 6 KO's in 7 turns if you don't bench them first." (search-summary quote, not read directly). The summary says both articles are about a Wigglytuff-based ("Insane Wiggly") deck, with the Phoenix version listing 3 instead of 4 Item Finders. So the quote is attributed to a Wigglytuff list aimed at Haymaker Pokemon ("70 HP hay pokemon" = Hitmonchan/Electabuzz/Scyther-type targets) and is worded conditionally ("if necessary", "if you don't bench them first"): a capability claim about one-hit KOs, not a played game. Note: the brief frames P4 loosely as Haymaker-related; the summary says Wigglytuff. Verify.
- P4 Phoenix report: Chad Mills, qualifier June 10-11, 2000; Wigglytuff list (Jigglypuff, Wigglytuff, Scyther, Chansey); plan was Wigglytuff on turn 2 and attacking early; 32 players in round 1, then 16; single elimination. Round results and opponents were not visible in the excerpt. [Phoenix report](https://www.pojo.com/KillerDecks/2000/June00/21u.htm) (via search summary)
- P2 (22a, "Super Trainer Showdown"): a search summary quotes a deck report: "Scyther is the best pokemon to start as your active, with build jiggly to wiggly with an energy and a DCE, RSA their energy removals, and have a turn 2 wiggly fully pumped", and "if you don't get a fast wiggly out, you have a very good chance to lose." Attribution to 22a specifically (vs another page) is not certain. [22a](https://www.pojo.com/KillerDecks/2000/August2000/22a.htm). This fits "oaked and had a wiggly koing one pokemon/turn by turn 2" being a best-case line (Oak draw, fast Wigglytuff), not an average.
- P6 (2ll, Oct 2000, 11-14 player): no search returned the page or the phrase "mow down six pokemon in seven turns". Nothing verified: deck, opponent, and whether it was a real game are unknown. [URL](https://www.pojo.com/KillerDecks/2000/October2000/2ll.htm)
- STS match format: single game, "a full 60-card deck, 6 prizes, and a 25 minute time limit"; 3 points for a decisive win, 2 for an adjudicated win at time, 1 draw; 6-8 Swiss rounds then top 8. [Pokumon STS 2000 page](https://pokumon.com/super-trainer-showdown-2000/) (read directly; recap site, not a 2000 primary source).

### Inferences
- A 25-minute limit with adjudicated wins implies many real games did not finish six Prizes; a 7-turn win was a best case, and time pressure favoured the fast decks.
- "6 KOs in 7 turns" with Item Finder recursion describes a sequence where every attack is a one-hit KO and Energy and PlusPower are available every turn. It is a ceiling, not a median.

### Gaps
- Full text of P4, P2, P6 (403 / archive denied). Per-game turns, minutes, Prize counts, opponents' decks, bench-out vs Prizes. Needs a browser read or another fetch route you approve.

## 2. Other 2000-era accounts of game length

### Takeaway
No source found gives turn counts or minutes for real Haymaker mirrors or Haymaker vs Wigglytuff games. Only qualitative speed claims.

### Cited Findings
- Aug 2000, Gym Heroes pre-release (35 players), Haymaker lists with 3 Scyther, 4 Electabuzz, 3 Hitmonchan, 4 Bill: [Haymaker With A Twist v1.3](https://pojo.com/KillerDecks/2000/August2000/10h.htm) (search summary; no turn data seen).
- Apr 2000 report: facing several Haymaker decks, one game "the closest game I have ever played", final Prize trade Ditto vs Wigglytuff; no turn count. [pojo April 2000](https://www.pojo.com/KillerDecks/2000/April2000/21h.htm) (search summary; page attribution uncertain)
- Apr 2000 report: "Got Wiggly turn two. ... made mince meat of his Clefairy's and Pikachu's." A real game with Wigglytuff on turn 2; opponent not Haymaker. (search summary; candidates [11g](https://www.pojo.com/KillerDecks/2000/April2000/11g.htm) or 21h)
- pojo STS Los Angeles feature (July 2000), per summary: field mostly Wigglytuff or Haymaker; the writer beat a Haymaker player by removing Energy then attacking with powered Mewtwos and Wigglytuffs. [pojo STS feature](https://pojo.com/Features/0600/0725STSStein.html) (search summary).
- PokeBeach forum, per summary: Plus Wiggly can do it on turn two while the earliest Arcanine can do 50 on turn 3 (post date unknown, probably later than 2000). [thread](https://www.pokebeach.com/forums/threads/haymaker-wigglytuff-raindance.93998/) (search summary; thread 403).
- Haymaker described as "start doing damage on the first turn" (no count). [pojo strategy, Feb 2000](https://pojo.com/TCGStrategies/February2000/28f.htm) (summary only).
- Retrospective (MODERN): 2000 matches "decided by how quickly ... the player on the receiving end" of Rocket's Sneak Attack could recover; Wigglytuff Sneak Attack can land one-hit KOs on Turn 2; Buzzap/Potpourri a first-turn KO against most Basics. No turn-to-win figures; uncited to 2000 sources. [jklaczpokemon](https://jklaczpokemon.com/2000-base-team-rocket/) (read directly)
- Base-set-only guide (MODERN): "The Haymaker mirror match is surprisingly fun and skillful!"; no game-length data. [jklaczpokemon](https://jklaczpokemon.com/base-set-decks/) (read directly)

### Inferences
- The 2000 speed literature is about first-KO and turn-2 Wigglytuff; none states a median game length.

### Gaps
- Surfaced but not read (403): Wizards in-house report (https://www.pojo.com/Features/0900/092100WotcChat.html), STS Los Angeles features, pojo reports 7d, 3l, 2d, 8k. Pokemon Gym archives, Beckett, Scrye, InQuest: not searched.

## 3. Energy Removal war and bench-outs

### Cited Findings
- (MODERN) Energy Removal and Super Energy Removal "deplete much of your opponent's Energy"; in Base-Rocket you "lose fewer games to deck out" than in Base-Fossil. [jklaczpokemon](https://jklaczpokemon.com/2000-base-team-rocket/)
- (MODERN) Gust of Wind and Energy Removal "prevent your opponent from setting up a Pokemon that can overpower you". [jklaczpokemon](https://jklaczpokemon.com/base-set-decks/)
- Andrew Marshall's winning California STS 2000 Masters list ran 2 Super Energy Removal and 2 Energy Removal. [ptcgeni.us](https://ptcgeni.us/decks/2000-haymaker/)

### Gaps
- No 2000 source on bench-out frequency vs six Prizes, or on the Energy Removal war's effect on game length.
