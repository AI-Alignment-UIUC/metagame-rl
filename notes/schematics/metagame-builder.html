Metagame Builder

The top of the three levels. It starts from random legal decks, plays them against each other with the trained pilot, and solves for an equilibrium mix of decks. It then searches for new decks that beat that mix and adds the ones that hold up in real games. What comes out is a predicted meta, scored against the archived 2000 field it never saw.

What the code does today

rl/psro.py already runs steps 1, 2, 3 and 5. For step 4 it trains a small RL edit policy (rl/builder.py, PPO over 16 remove-and-add edits). The log #27 plan drops that policy in favor of plain search with restarts and edit budgets. It comes back only if plain search stops finding new support decks. The distance rule currently uses 0.1 (6 cards); the plan raises it to about 10. Neither the value feature nor the ensemble is in rl/matchup.py yet.

Why a model in the loop

Real games are the expensive part: about 16–19 games/s with the token pilot. Ranking candidates with the matchup model and confirming only the top five costs about 20× less per step than testing 100 candidates with games. The model only ranks. Every number in the matrix comes from real games, so a model error costs one wasted confirmation, not a wrong meta.

Is exploration working?

  • Cold starts from different seeds reach overlapping supports, by card overlap.
  • New decks keep entering the support, and the share of the pool tried keeps growing.
  • The model's error on newly tested decks stays small.

Baseline so far

The only equilibrium computed so far is SimpleBot's, over the 24 human lists, with 100 games per pair (about ±10 points per cell). Its support is a three-deck cycle: Bartlett Sponge, Diamond Articuno/Hitmonchan/Scyther and Pratt Wigglytuff. These are the three decks hardest to counter (best counters 57%, 63% and 65%). Most other Wigglytuff and Haymaker lists have a counter that wins 85–93%. The trained-policy matrix and a cold-start population come next.
