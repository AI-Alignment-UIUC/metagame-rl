# The "six KOs in seven turns" figure is a ceiling, not a median

No 2000 source gives a typical game length for Haymaker or Wigglytuff, so the notes cannot say whether our 19-32 own turns to six Prizes is representative. The only number in circulation, "six KOs in seven turns", is a best-case capability claim from Wigglytuff deck notes, not a measured game. It is worded conditionally ("if necessary", "if you don't bench them first"), and the second source implies the same: the deck wins fast only with a quick Wigglytuff. The third cited page (2ll) describes a real game, but it is a Mewtwo stall deck, not Haymaker or Wigglytuff. This is a weak basis for calling our engine slow. It cannot show that our median is right either. All the evidence comes from search-engine summaries, because every pojo.com page returned HTTP 403 and the Wayback Machine was blocked by the permission system. No page was read in full. Treat the figure as low-to-medium firmness and as an upper bound on speed.

## What the three pages say, as far as it could be seen

The phrase appears in the Wigglytuff deck descriptions of pages 21u (June 2000, Chad Mills's Phoenix qualifier report) and 22a (August 2000). A search summary quotes it as: "with pluspowers you take out the 70 HP hay pokemon in one hit, and Item finders can get back the pluspowers if necessary to get 6 KO's in 7 turns if you don't bench them first" ([21u](https://www.pojo.com/KillerDecks/2000/June00/21u.htm), [22a](https://www.pojo.com/KillerDecks/2000/August2000/22a.htm)). That is a deck-building argument. It says PlusPower plus Item Finder recursion lets the deck one-hit Haymaker's 70 HP attackers, so six Prizes in seven turns is feasible. It is not a game log, so it records no opponent, deck or result. The phrase "if you don't bench them first" reads as a caveat about the opponent benching Pokémon, which would stop one-hit KOs from keeping pace. It does not say whether bench-outs count toward the seven turns. The 21u version lists 3 Item Finders and the 22a version lists 4. The June report also states the plan of Wigglytuff on turn 2 and attacking early, and a second summary of 22a quotes "if you don't get a fast wiggly out, you have a very good chance to lose". A fourth line, "I oaked and had a wiggly koing one pokemon/turn by turn 2", is from a best-case start with Oak. Which page each line belongs to is not certain (summary only).

Page 2ll (October 2000, an 11-14 player event) was never surfaced by any search. Our earlier research (C02-round-length) read it and quotes "I then proceed to mow down six pokemon in seven turns". It identified the deck as a Mewtwo "active stall" list and the game as an actual one. This notes folder neither confirms nor contradicts that, so I treat the quote as plausible but unverified here. Even taken at face value, that deck is not Haymaker or Wigglytuff, and the speaker was describing the best game of an event, not an average. Whether bench-outs were counted is unknown for all three.

## Why the figure does not transfer to our simulator

The claim is about the owner's own turns, and the earlier report converted it to roughly 13-14 engine turns. It is a goldfish-style ceiling in which every attack is a one-hit KO, an Energy and a PlusPower are available each turn, and the opponent does not retaliate or bench out. Our 19-32 own-turn medians are against a passive opponent, the other extreme, so the two numbers are not directly comparable. The gap is 3-4x, but part of it should be expected because we report medians of all four human lists and the claim is a best case for one archetype. Three of the four lists are Haymaker, and the cited claim is about Wigglytuff. No source in the notes gives any Haymaker turn count. The Haymaker material says only that the deck damages "on the first turn", and that is a qualitative claim ([pojo strategy, Feb 2000](https://pojo.com/TCGStrategies/February2000/28f.htm), summary only).

## What other evidence exists, and how thin it is

Nothing in the notes gives per-game turns or minutes. The tournament format is the only hard constraint. A single game, 60 cards, six Prizes and a 25-minute limit were recorded on a recap site, with 3 points for a win, 2 for an adjudicated win at time and 1 for a draw ([Pokumon STS 2000](https://pokumon.com/super-trainer-showdown-2000/)). The earlier STS references put the official limit at 20 minutes. Adjudicated wins existed because many games did not finish, which fits a slow median. It also shows that fast decks gained from time pressure. The retrospectives say Wigglytuff Sneak Attack could land one-hit KOs on turn 2 and that Buzzap or Potpourri could KO most Basics on turn 1, but they give no win turn and are modern sources ([jklaczpokemon](https://jklaczpokemon.com/2000-base-team-rocket/)). A winning California list ran 2 Super Energy Removal and 2 Energy Removal ([ptcgeni.us](https://ptcgeni.us/decks/2000-haymaker/)), which suggests Energy-denial games slowed real play. No 2000 source quantifies that, nor bench-out frequency, cards drawn per turn or bench sizes.

## What would settle it

Read 21u, 22a, 2ll and the July 2000 STS Los Angeles features in a browser, or approve a route around the 403s and the archive block. Then look for games with an actual turn or minute count, ideally Haymaker mirrors and Haymaker vs Wigglytuff. Independent of the sources, the simulator can settle most of it. Play scripted best-case lines (turn-2 Wigglytuff with Oak, PlusPower on every attack, no opponent bench) and check whether the engine reaches six Prizes in about 7 own turns. If it does, the engine's flow is fine and the human lists' 19-32 turns reflect deck consistency and the passive-opponent setup. If it cannot, the engine has a pace problem that no source is needed to prove.

## Conclusion

The figure supports only one statement: a Wigglytuff deck could, in ideal conditions, claim six Prizes in about seven turns. It cannot be used as a target median, and it should not be cited as evidence that the engine is slow. A 3-4x gap between a ceiling and a median is unremarkable. The decisive test is the best-case replay in our own engine, and it is cheap.
