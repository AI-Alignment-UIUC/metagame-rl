# C02 findings: round length and time procedure of the 2000 STS (Long Beach, 22 July 2000)

Outside research for `record.md` (2026-10-22, about 21:50–22:45; one research agent; no runs). The agent could not
write files, so the main session saved this text from its report (log #164). The agent checked each quote against the
page or file it fetched. Three items are scans it read by eye: the East Coast booklet, the qualifier pamphlet and the
Beckett article. Old wizards.com pages come from the Wayback Machine. **The second agent's quote check that the plan
called for was not run.** Which decks won was not used for any number.

**Summary.**
- **R = 1,200 s is documented:** four WotC documents and three independent player reports from the event agree.
- **The time procedure is documented:**
  - the turn in progress is finished, then Prizes are compared;
  - more Prizes taken wins for 2 points (a full win is 3);
  - equal Prizes is a draw, 1 point each;
  - every Swiss round is one game.
- **s and O are not documented.** s has no primary source and no two independent sources. With the record's priors
  the formula gives T in [17, 57]. Under the record's mapping this is **R-unknown**, so T is drawn uniform on 20..45.
- **Two other Q2 numbers are documented by at least two sources:**
  - time was reached in about 5–9% of Swiss games in the 10-and-under and 11–14 divisions, and in about 15–25% in
    15-and-over;
  - fast human decks took six Prizes in about 7 of their own turns (about 13–14 engine turns), which supports
    **H-pilot-slow**.

Other notes:
- **Qualifiers (Mall Tour, single elimination) also used 20 minutes.** [A7] "Each Tournament will consist of up to five
  20-minute rounds using 6 prize cards per game." [P3] "Standard tourney rules also apply, 20 minutes per round."
- **Qualifiers handled time differently from the STS.** A Prize tie went to whoever had more cards in their deck
  ([P3]; [P14] "Derk beats me by having more cards in his deck (because we were tied with prizes)."). At least one
  qualifier judge stopped the game at once: [P14] "TIME! What? Don't I get to finish my turn, I ask. The master trainer
  says when time is called, it's over." The STS judge, by contrast, let the turn finish [P1].
- **DCI store events in 1999 also used 20 minutes.** [P10] "time was called right in the nick of time for me after 20
  minutes … ignoring the DCI-enforced time limit rule". [P11] "Then when time ran out (20 minute time limit) we both had
  2 prizes left, so we went to sudden death." The DCI floor rules themselves recommended a longer round: [A5] §212
  "Constructed and Limited tournaments—twenty-five minutes".
- **pokumon.com is wrong for Long Beach.** Its STS page [S1] says "a 25 minute time limit" for both events. Its scan
  [A4] comes from the East Coast booklet, which lists Gym Heroes and Gym Challenge.

**Standings method.** In the Swiss a decisive win scores 3 and a loss at time scores 0, so any total that is not a
multiple of 3 means at least one win at time (2) or draw (1).
- **Data:** pokumon.com reproduces the full final standings for all six divisions of the two 2000 STS events [S1].
- **Cross-checks:** the Long Beach division sizes (147, 222, 180) match Beckett's counts [A6] exactly. The reporters'
  own totals also match: Stein 19, Mills 17, Viray 16 (5 wins, 1 draw and 1 loss).
- **Model:** each game goes to time with probability f. A share q of timed games are draws (q = 0–0.3), the rest are
  2–0 wins at time, and each player wins half their games.
- **Fit:** f was fitted to players with at least 9 points, to limit the bias from players who dropped. 7 rounds were
  used for 10U and 8 for the other divisions.
- **Caveats:** the model is crude. It assumes independent games, and a loss at time is invisible in the totals. A
  simpler bound (3 × pairings − total points) is unusable because players dropped out.

## 5. Implied T and rival

T = (R − O) / s with R = 1,200 s:
- a = (1,200 − 180) / 60 = 1,020 / 60 = **17**
- b = (1,200 − 60) / 20 = 1,140 / 20 = **57**
- If O = 0 (one source), b = 60.
- For reference only: with the East Coast's 25 minutes the range would be 22–72.

**Rival: the evidence supports R-unknown.**
- s has no primary source and fewer than two independent sources bounding it, so under the record's mapping T is
  drawn uniform on 20..45.
- **H-pilot-slow is supported:** human Prize wins took about 13–14 engine turns, against 34–41 for our lists.
- **R-fast and R-slow cannot be separated:** the T range straddles both bands. Time was reached in about 1 game in 4–6
  for 15+ and about 1 in 12–20 for the younger divisions.

## 6. How the cut should be applied

1. **Finish the turn in progress, then decide on Prizes, with no extra turns.** This is the record's default, and it is
   confirmed by DCI §216 [A5] and by the STS judge [P1]. Strictly, a player is "in mid-turn" only after drawing for the
   turn. Do not import the modern +3-turns rule [M1]. Some qualifiers stopped at once instead [P14], but that is not
   STS practice.
2. **A Prize tie at the cut is a draw** (1 point each), with no sudden death in the Swiss. The engine needs a three-way
   result.
3. **A win at time is worth 2, not 3.** The faithful scoring is decisive win 3, win at time 2, draw 1, loss 0.
   Bench-outs and deck-outs are full wins. Scoring a Prize lead at T as a full win departs from 2000 practice.
4. **Cut the Swiss only.** The finals "last until one player wins" [A1]. When emulating top-cut games, use no cut or
   the DCI play-on-until-ahead rule [A5]. This rests on one source (medium-low).
5. **R is the same in every division, but how often time was reached differs** (about 5–9% against 15–25%). This
   matters if the human lists come from one division.
6. **Not applicable to the engine:** the late penalty ([P1] "any player late to a match loses 1 prize. An additional
   prize is lost for each 5 minutes that the player is late."), judge time extensions and the qualifiers' deck-count
   tiebreak.
